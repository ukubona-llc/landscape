The Framework

Pentad of Optimization

Public health treated as a dynamic learning system. The map is not decoration — it is a visualization of the loss function. Each iteration brings the landscape closer to the clinical ideal.

01
θt

State Selection

The dropdown menu. The user selects the disease burden to analyze — CKD, Depression, Diabetes. This defines the parameter space for the current iteration.

02
L(θ)

Loss Function

Reality minus Ideal. Quantifies the public health debt for the selected condition — both in documented prevalence and in data deserts where literature is absent.

03
∇L

Gradient Discovery

The ROYGBIV heat map. Identifies the direction and magnitude of steepest need — which districts require intervention most urgently, where the data gaps are sharpest.

04
−η∇L

The Update Rule

The grant-funded intervention. Research resources, student-led data collection, and policy shifts applied in the direction of the negative gradient to reduce loss.

05
θt+1

New State

The updated landscape. Reduced data deserts, measurably improved health reality — and the baseline for the next grant cycle. The loop is self-sustaining.

θt  →  L(θ)  →  ∇L  →  −η∇L  →  θt+1   // each grant cycle is one iteration
First Case Study

Chronic Kidney Disease & ESRD in Uganda

While the atlas is designed for multi-condition mapping, the collaboration with UCU opens on Chronic Kidney Disease and End-Stage Renal Disease — the sharpest intersection of Dr. Muzaale's NIH-funded expertise and Uganda's most undercharted health terrain.

Renal replacement therapy infrastructure is sparse and geographically uneven. The data landscape is even thinner. That combination makes CKD/ESRD the ideal first optimization target: high clinical stakes, high gradient, high grant viability.

The methodology is direct: systematic literature review of PubMed and AJOL, district-level extraction, prevalence standardization, geospatial mapping — then submission. The platform already exists. The loop just needs data to run.
